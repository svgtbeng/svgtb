            Stage 3 of 6 · Read

            The story in simple English

            Read the story about the mission. The highlighted words are difficult. Then find a simple word for each one.

            A tiny moon, a big mission

            In October 2026, a big rocket took off from a small island in the south of Japan. It carried a spacecraft called MMX. Japan built the spacecraft, and France and Germany built a small rover for it. It is a groundbreaking mission: no spacecraft has ever landed on a moon of Mars, so MMX wants to be the first.

            Mars has two small moons, Phobos and Deimos. An American astronomer, Asaph Hall, discovered them in 1877. Their names come from Greek and mean “fear” and “terror”. These moons are not like our Moon. They are tiny and irregularly shaped: they look more like potatoes than balls. Phobos is only 27 kilometres wide, so its gravity is very weak.

            The journey to Mars and back is very long. MMX will reach Mars in 2027. Then it will fly around Phobos for about three years and gather information about it.

            First, MMX will drop a little rover called Idefix. The engineers named it after the tenacious little dog from the Asterix comics. Idefix is about as big as a microwave. On Earth it weighs 25 kilograms, but on Phobos it will weigh only about 15 grams! When it hits the ground, it will bounce a few times. Then it will drive autonomously, because a signal from Earth needs up to 40 minutes to get there and back. Idefix will examine the ground and help scientists find a good place for the landing.

            After that, the main spacecraft will land on Phobos for only two and a half hours and take a sample of soil. Then it will start its long way home. In 2031, a small capsule with the sample will hurtle towards Earth and land in the Australian desert.

            Why is this so important? Scientists still don’t know where Phobos came from. Maybe it was an asteroid, and Mars caught it a long time ago. Or maybe a giant space rock hit Mars and ejected material into space, and this material became Phobos. The sample will help us find the answer. And there is a bonus: Phobos dust probably has a little bit of Mars in it too. Landing on such a tiny moon so far away is a real feat. For space fans, the whole mission is mind-blowing.

              Stage 4 of 6 · Grammar

              Past Simple: it happened!

              We use the Past Simple for finished actions in the past: yesterday, in 1961, last year, in October 2026.

              The rule

              + regular

              verb + -ed: land → landed, carry → carried, stop → stopped
              The spacecraft landed on the Moon.

              + irregular

              learn them by heart: fly → flew, build → built, take → took
              Gagarin flew into space in 1961.

              − negative

              didn’t + verb: The rover didn’t need a driver. not “didn’t needed”

              ? question

              Did + subject + verb: Did the rocket take off? — Yes, it did. / No, it didn’t.

              can → could

              In the past, people couldn’t bring soil from Mars.

              Careful!

              «Люди никогда не были на Марсе» = People have never been to Mars. This is Present Perfect: we don’t say when.

              In the Past Simple, we say or know when: Nobody went to Mars in the 20th century.

                      Stage 6 of 6 · Talk

                      Let’s discuss

                      Answer in full sentences. Questions with the tag need the Past Simple.

                      1. What was the most interesting fact in the video for you? Past Simple
                      2. Did you know that Mars has two moons? What did you know about Mars before this lesson? Past Simple
                      3. Why do you think scientists want a piece of Phobos?
                      4. Idefix will work alone, very far from people. Would you like to drive a rover on another planet? Why or why not?
                      5. When did you last look at the night sky? What did you see? Past Simple
                      6. Is it a good idea to spend a lot of money on space missions? Why or why not?
                      7. The capsule will come back in 2031. How old will you be then? What will be different in your life?
                      8. You can give a name to the next rover. What name will you choose, and why?
